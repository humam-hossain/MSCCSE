The recommendation in brief

Take CSE752 and CSE717 in semester 1. Put CSE791 in the semester where thesis work begins. Choose Plan C if the thesis can be split into CSE700A and CSE700B, or Plan B if it must be one 12-credit block.

Why every plan needs at least four semesters

After your 6-credit first semester, 30 credits remain. At 12 credits per semester at most, that takes at least three more semesters (12 + 12 + 6). The 12-credit plans don’t finish sooner than the 9-credit ones; they only change where the load falls.

My recommendation: Plan C, with Plan B as the fallback

Research output is what PhD committees weigh most, and Plan C is the only four-semester plan that gives the thesis two semesters. If the thesis must be one block, Plan B is next best, because its light semester 3 works as a preparation semester.

Semester 2 in both is four courses, but three of them are mathematical, which is where you’re strongest. If that still feels like too much risk to your CGPA, choose Plan D when an extra semester is affordable, or Plan A when it isn’t.

Choosing a thesis direction

You don’t need to decide now. Decide during your CSE791 semester, once you’ve seen most of the field.

By then every plan will have shown you deep learning, vision-language models and language models. Plans B and C also cover RL first; Plans A and D bring it in alongside or just before CSE791.

Use course projects as test drives

A project in CSE754 or CSE753 that resembles a possible thesis tells you whether you enjoy the area, gives you pilot results, and shows a potential supervisor what you can do.

Choose the supervisor before the topic

An actively publishing supervisor whose interests overlap with yours matters more than the exact research question. Their letter will also be the most important one in your PhD application.

Making a late thesis work

Keeping the thesis at the end is fine, as long as the groundwork starts earlier.

The risk of a one-semester thesis

In Plans A and B, the thesis gets one registered semester, about 14 class weeks. That is short for publishable research, so do the groundwork informally beforehand.

  1. Semester 2: find peopleShortlist two or three potential supervisors and read their recent papers.
  2. Semester 2: test an areaShape a course project, ideally in CSE754 or CSE753, around a possible thesis topic.
  3. Semester before the thesis: agree the topicConfirm the supervisor and topic, and write the proposal in CSE791.
  4. Semester before the thesis: get a running startReproduce one baseline paper and set up compute (department GPUs, Kaggle or Colab).
  5. Thesis semester: executeRun the main experiments in roughly the first eight weeks and write in the last six.

Check where deadlines fall in your plan

Many US PhD deadlines fall in early to mid December for entry the following autumn. Contact potential advisors two to three months before, and request letters four to six weeks before the first deadline. If your paper won’t be ready in time, a research-assistant year after the MSc is common and well regarded.
