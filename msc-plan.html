Why every plan needs at least four semesters

After your 6-credit first semester, 30 credits remain. At a maximum of 12 credits per semester, that takes at least three more semesters (12 + 12 + 6). The 12-credit plans therefore don’t finish sooner than the 9-credit ones; they only change where the load falls.

The reasoning behind each course

CSE752 Deep Neural Networks

Covers backpropagation, MLPs, CNNs, RNNs, autoencoders, VAEs and GANs, transformers (BERT and GPT), optimizers including SGD, Adam and learning-rate schedules, dropout and batch normalization, ResNets and transfer learning.

It is the base for CSE751, CSE754, CSE755 and CSE753, and nearly every AI paper assumes this material. Assessment is likely implementation and derivation, and ethics is only one line in the objectives. That makes it the first course to take.

CSE717 Mathematical Programming

Constrained optimization and Lagrange multipliers, linear programming and the Simplex method, duality and complementary slackness, interior-point methods, integer, quadratic and nonlinear programming, and dynamic programming.

Duality appears in SVMs, constrained RL and LLM alignment objectives. Quadratic programming sits inside model-predictive control, which CSE753 covers. Dynamic programming leads directly to the Bellman equations in RL. It’s pure problem-solving, your strongest area.

Gap to fill: there is no convex analysis or theory of why SGD converges. Read chapters 2–5 of Boyd and Vandenberghe’s Convex Optimization alongside it.

CSE756 Probabilistic Graphical Models

Bayesian networks, Markov random fields, d-separation, variable elimination, belief propagation, junction trees, MCMC and Gibbs sampling, HMMs, Kalman filters, CRFs, variational inference, neural ODEs and graph neural networks.

Variational inference is the mathematics behind VAEs and diffusion models, so this course makes CSE755 much easier. Kalman filters are standard for robot state estimation, and the GNN content gives you some of the graph-based thinking you wanted.

CSE754 Deep Learning for Computer Vision

CNN architectures, detection and segmentation, Vision Transformers, multimodal models (CLIP, BLIP, Flamingo, LLaVA), image generation, 3D vision and NeRF, video transformers, self-supervised learning, and a project.

This is the only course that teaches VLMs directly, and it is the perception half of robotics. The project is implementation work rather than essays. Design it as a test drive for a possible thesis topic.

CSE751 Advanced Natural Language Processing

Embeddings, parsing, sequence models and the Viterbi algorithm, RNNs, attention, transformers (byte-pair encoding, positional encoding, query-key-value), BERT, LLMs, state space models, and generative and ethical NLP.

It is the only course centred on LLMs, the dominant technology in AI today, and VLMs are half language model.

Watch out: one official objective is academic reading and writing, and the syllabus ends with ethics. Ask seniors how much of the grade comes from paper reviews and reports. If it’s a lot, swap in CSE708.

Not covered: LoRA fine-tuning, instruction tuning, RLHF and DPO, and retrieval-augmented generation. Learn these from papers.

CSE755 Generative Machine Learning

Latent variable models, autoregressive models, VAEs, GANs, diffusion and score-based models, energy-based models, normalizing flows, and transformer-based and multimodal generation.

The most mathematical of the modern-AI courses. Diffusion models power image and video generation and are increasingly used as robot control policies. It builds directly on CSE756.

CSE753 Advanced Reinforcement Learning

MDPs, DQN variants, policy gradients and actor-critic, PPO and TRPO, model-based RL with world models and MPC, multi-agent and hierarchical RL, inverse RL, safe RL and imitation learning.

Central to robotics. The same policy-optimization ideas are used to align LLMs and train reasoning models. The mathematics (Bellman equations, the policy gradient theorem) suits you.

Reserve: CSE708 Graph Theory

Eulerian and Hamiltonian graphs, trees and counting, planarity, colouring, digraphs, Markov chains, matching, Hall’s and Menger’s theorems, and matroids. It’s pure, timeless discrete mathematics. Swap it in for CSE751 if CSE751 turns out to be writing-heavy. It uses one of your two outside slots, so confirm approval first, and remember that proof-based exams can be demanding too.

My recommendation: Plan C, with Plan B as the fallback

Research output is what PhD committees weigh most, and Plan C is the only four-semester plan that gives the thesis two semesters. If the thesis must be one block, Plan B is the next best, because its light semester 3 works as a preparation semester.

Semester 2 in both is four courses, but three of them are mathematical, which is where you’re strongest. If that still feels like too much risk to your CGPA, choose Plan D when an extra semester is affordable, or Plan A when it isn’t.

Choosing a thesis direction

You don’t need to decide now. Decide during your CSE791 semester. By then every plan will have shown you deep learning, vision-language models and language models. Plans B and C also cover RL first; Plans A and D bring it in alongside or just before CSE791.

Use course projects as test drives

A project in CSE754 or CSE753 that resembles a possible thesis tells you whether you enjoy the area, gives you pilot results, and shows a potential supervisor what you can do.

Choose the supervisor before the topic

An actively publishing supervisor whose interests overlap with yours matters more than the exact research question. Their letter will also be the most important one in your PhD application.

Making a late thesis work

The risk of a one-semester thesis

In Plans A and B, the thesis gets one registered semester, about 14 class weeks. That is short for publishable research. You can keep the thesis at the end and still protect its quality by doing the groundwork earlier, informally.

  1. Semester 2: find peopleShortlist two or three potential supervisors and read their recent papers.
  2. Semester 2: test an areaShape a course project, ideally in CSE754 or CSE753, around a possible thesis topic.
  3. Semester before the thesis: agree the topicConfirm the supervisor and topic, and write the proposal in CSE791.
  4. Semester before the thesis: get a running startReproduce one baseline paper and set up compute (department GPUs, Kaggle or Colab).
  5. Thesis semester: executeRun the main experiments in roughly the first eight weeks and write in the last six.

Check where deadlines fall in your plan

Many US PhD deadlines fall in early to mid December for entry the following autumn. Contact potential advisors two to three months before, and request letters four to six weeks before the first deadline. If your paper won’t be ready in time, a research-assistant year after the MSc is common and well regarded.
